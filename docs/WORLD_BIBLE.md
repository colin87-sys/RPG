# AETHERWIND SAGA — World Bible

Binding narrative constitution. Names, palettes, speech registers and arcs
here are contractual: `characters/roster.js`, `story/*`, `battle/espers`,
and `world/zones` must match this document exactly. Original work — this
evokes the golden-age JRPG register; it copies no one.

---

## 1. The world of Erevane

**Name:** Erevane — "the land beneath the Ring".

**Premise.** Eight centuries ago the moon — which the old faith called the
Sleeping God, Lunareth — shattered in a night remembered as the Sundering.
Its shards never fell. They hang in a slow glowing ring across the sky, and
from them blows the **Aetherwind**: a current of anima, the soul-stuff of the
dead god, drifting down like weather. Where the wind pools, crops grow twice
over, the sick recover, and stone remembers being carved. Civilisation
rebuilt itself around the pooling-places and learned to breathe the wind into
craft: this is magic.

**The magic system and its cost.** Anima is not free energy — it is memory
given motion. To cast, a mage draws the Aetherwind through their own
remembered life; the spell takes shape from a memory and *spends* it. Small
spells cost small things — the smell of a kitchen, the second verse of a
song. Great magic costs faces, names, years. Spent memory crystallises and
falls from the caster as **glasspetals**: small translucent leaves, faintly
warm, each one a moment no one will ever have again. Mages are recognisable
by what they've forgotten. The empire industrialised the exchange: the
**Choir Engines** of Virelia burn *harvested* anima siphoned from the shards
themselves — and, as the party will learn, the shards are not batteries.
They are the god's unfinished dream, and the dream is running out. This is
the melancholy at the heart of everything: **every wonder in Erevane is paid
for out of a finite account of remembrance**, and the civilisation on screen
is gorgeous precisely because it is spending its last inheritance.

**The central conflict.** The Cantorate of Virelia — the choral empire that
administers the wind — has begun drilling the last intact great shard, the
**Vesper Shard**, to feed its engines for another century. The provinces
starve of wind so the capital can blaze. Lord-Cantor Casimir Veyre, the
empire's own First Voice, has concluded the only moral act left is to still
the Aetherwind forever — ending magic, dropping the sky-parishes, and
freeing every soul the wind is quietly strip-mining. The party begins the
game trying to stop the drill, and ends it caught between a world that
cannot live without the wind and a god who cannot die while it blows.

**Why the world is visually distinctive.** Two light sources with two
temperaments: a warm mortal sun and the cold teal ring of the broken moon,
so every scene is lit by life and afterlife at once. Spent magic is
*visible* and *falls* — glasspetal drifts pile in gutters, blow across
battlefields, and glow faintly where great spells were cast generations ago.
Architecture is choral: the Cantorate builds in resonant curves, organ-pipe
towers and bell-shaped vaults designed to sing when the wind moves. See
ART_BIBLE.md — the entire palette derives from this premise.

## 2. Main arc — five chapters

**Chapter I — "The Petalfall Levy."** The Cantorate comes to the border
province of Hallow's Reach to conscript memories — a levy of anima taken
from the living to prime the Vesper drill. Auren, a deserter hiding as a
harvest guard, fails to stop the levy; the village's elders are left
smiling and empty. He flees with Seren, an oracle the levy inexplicably
*could not read*, and the party forms around getting her to the Hanging
Abbey, the last free sanctum. Inciting wound: the player watches a named
NPC forget the player's party mid-sentence. Ends with the Abbey burning
and the first Esper, Cindrath, bending its antlers to Seren.

**Chapter II — "The Undersea Road."** Crossing the vitrified ocean —
the Undersea of Glass — to raise the provinces against the drill. The
party gathers Bramm (who built the drill and defected), Kite (who runs
the glass-rails), and Emrys. Rising action as heist-and-rally: stealing a
Choir Engine's anima manifest, which proves the harvest is taken from
*living* provinces, not the shards alone. Ends with Veyre, met in person,
letting the party escape with the manifest — because publishing it serves
him. First unease: the antagonist keeps *helping*.

**Chapter III — "The Six Anchors."** Armed with the manifest, the party
undertakes what every prophecy and ally urges: reawaken the six Anchor
Choirs, ancient stations that once "regulated" the wind, to starve the
capital's engines and halt the drill. Triumphal chapter — each Anchor is a
dungeon, an Esper, a province rallied. At the sixth Anchor, Yshara joins,
warning that her tribe's songs call the Anchors "the god's shackles." She
is dismissed. **The turn the player will not see coming:** with all six
Anchors singing, the ring flares — and Veyre's voice thanks the party
across the sky. The Anchors were never regulators; they are the locks on
Lunareth's sleep, and they can only be *disengaged* while singing. The
party has spent two chapters completing the antagonist's ritual for him.
The chapter ends with the Vesper Shard cracking, the Aetherwind faltering
world-wide — and Seren remembering, all at once, what she is: the dreaming
heart of Lunareth, incarnated to decide whether the god wakes or dies.

**Chapter IV — "The Stilling."** The dark chapter. Magic gutters; sky-
parishes are evacuated or fall; the party is scattered and hunted, and
each member's personal wound is forced open (see arcs below). The party
reunites in the ashlands and confronts the true choice: let Veyre still
the wind (every mage keeps what memory remains, the god dies in its sleep,
the world goes dark and ordinary), or wake Lunareth (the wind becomes a
gale — and Seren, the dream's heart, is unmade with the dream). Veyre
offers the party amnesty and means it. Ends with the march on Moonwake
and the descent into the Wound.

**Chapter V — "Aubade."** (An aubade is a song at parting, at dawn.)
The synthesis the prophecies missed: a god need not wake *or* die — it can
be **mourned**. Using the six Espers as a choir and Seren as its voice, the
party performs the Sundering's unfinished funeral rite inside the Vesper
Shard while Veyre — half ally, half final boss, fighting to guarantee the
end he has bled for — tries to silence the song. The rite lets Lunareth
finish dying *awake*: the ring dims to embers, the Aetherwind becomes
seasonal and gentle — magic survives, small and honest, priced at what a
person can afford to forget. Seren survives as the memory the god chose to
leave behind: fully mortal, and slowly filling with a life of her own.
Final image: dawn on the Glasspetal Fields, the ring faded to a pale arc,
the party walking out of frame — the art bible's hero shot, at last in
warm light.

## 3. The six playable characters

Palettes are contractual for `roster.js` and costume generation. "Want vs
need" is the engine of party conflict: each character wants something that
cuts against what the party must do.

### 3.1 Auren Vael — the deserter knight
- **Age/class:** 24. Skysworn Blade (balanced sword vanguard; limit: time-slowed iaijutsu chain).
- **Silhouette:** tall, straight-backed but head slightly bowed; long asymmetric coat split for the sword-draw, one pauldron only (left), high collar. Reads as a vertical line broken at the hip by the sheathed sword.
- **Palette:** slate `#3D4A5C`, worn amber `#C9924A`, undercoat `#1E262E`, steel `#B8BFC7`.
- **Weapon:** "Daybreak", a bastard sword of pale moonglass that fogs with his breath.
- **Wound:** as a Cantorate officer he executed a levy on his own home village; his mother thanked him, smiling, having already forgotten him.
- **Want vs need:** he wants to *atone by punishment* — to be spent, to die usefully; the party needs him to lead, which means surviving and being forgiven.
- **Speech register:** clipped, formal, drops articles when tense; never uses "I" when giving orders. *"Wall's coming down. Take Seren. Don't look back for me — that's an order, not a request."*
- **Arc:** I: runs from command. II: commands by accident, hates it. III: believes in the Anchors hardest — the turn is *his* failure. IV: offered Veyre's amnesty and, for one night, takes it. V: chooses to live; his limit gains a golden dawn tint (`HALLOW` fringe) from Chapter V on.

### 3.2 Seren Lys — the unread oracle
- **Age/class:** 19 (apparent). Oracle (white magic, sung; limit: "Aubade" — full-party memory-restoration surge).
- **Silhouette:** small, wind-caught: layered shawl-robes that always move (cloth sim mandatory), bare feet, hair a pale drifting mass twice the width of her body. Reads as a candle-flame shape.
- **Palette:** ivory `#EAE2D4`, teal `#5FB8B0`, gold thread `#FFC24D`, skin cool-pale.
- **Weapon:** chime-staff "Vesperine" — seven suspended glass bells; casting rings them.
- **Wound:** no memories before age twelve, and — the quiet horror — casting costs her nothing. She has nothing of her own to spend; she is spending the god's.
- **Want vs need:** she wants an ordinary life's worth of memories — to accumulate a self; the party needs her to be the ritual's voice, which means giving that self up.
- **Speech register:** unhurried, concrete images, present tense even about the past; asks questions no one else would. *"Your mother is still in the room where she forgot you, Auren. Rooms remember. Shall we go and ask it?"*
- **Arc:** I: a mystery to herself. II: collects "small treasures" (a recipe, a rude song) — borrowed memories. III: the remembering — she is the dream's heart. IV: withdraws, speaks of herself in third person. V: sings the aubade *and survives it*, mortal, empty, and free; her final line is the game's first "I".

### 3.3 Bramm Okkonen — the engineer of the wound
- **Age/class:** 51. Forgemaster (heavy tank; arm-mounted piston-cannon; limit: overcharged seismic slam).
- **Silhouette:** a keg on bowed legs; leather apron over bare scarred shoulder, the right arm replaced below the elbow by his own brass-and-steel piston rig. Reads as a triangle, widest at the belt.
- **Palette:** iron `#4A4440`, brass `#B8863B`, oxblood apron `#6B3328`, ash-grey beard.
- **Weapon:** "Second Regret", the piston-cannon — its first iteration was the Vesper drill's bore-head.
- **Wound:** he designed the drill as a *survey* engine; the Cantorate weaponised it, and its first ignition emptied his whole work-crew — thirty names he keeps in a ledger he can no longer match to faces.
- **Want vs need:** he wants to *unmake* his machine — smash the drill and every schematic; the party needs the drill intact in Chapter V, re-tuned into the rite's resonator. The engineer must finish his terrible machine.
- **Speech register:** slow, load-bearing sentences; measures everything twice; endearments as insults. *"Torque it past the red line, lad, and it'll sing — once. Everything sings once. The trick is building things that get a second verse."*
- **Arc:** I–II: penance through maintenance. III: builds the Anchor interfaces — complicit again. IV: reads the thirty names aloud at the Wound, the game's quietest scene. V: rebuilds the drill into the organ that saves the world; keeps the ledger.

### 3.4 Kirella "Kite" Marrow — the glass-rail corsair
- **Age/class:** 26. Corsair (speed/evasion, thrown moonglass chakram, steal; limit: ricochet storm off frozen time-shards).
- **Silhouette:** all diagonals: cropped storm-coat snapping behind her, one long boot-blade, chakram carried across the back like a halo knocked askew.
- **Palette:** storm-blue `#2E4A5F`, bleached rope `#D9CBA6`, signal-red sash `#C9403A` (the party's only red — earned, see below), dark skin, salt-white tattoos.
- **Weapon:** "Hallelujah", a rim-weighted chakram cut from her old ship's keel-glass.
- **Wound:** she sold her smuggler crew's route to a Cantorate levy to buy her little sister's exemption — and the levy took the sister anyway. The red sash is her sister's; wearing it is a sentence she's serving.
- **Want vs need:** she wants her sister's memories *back* — and Veyre's engines can, horrifyingly, return harvested anima to a body; the party's need (still the engines) destroys her only hope. She is the member most tempted to betray, and in Chapter IV she does — and comes back.
- **Speech register:** fast, transactional, jokes as armour, never says "sorry" — says "noted". *"Trust is a cargo, sunshine — heavy, spoils fast, and I've seen what people pay for it. …Fine. Load it up."*
- **Arc:** I–II: mercenary, funny, closed. III: starts leaving the sash off. IV: takes Veyre's trade, sees the "restored" sister is a puppet of borrowed moments, and breaks the engine herself. V: gives the sash to the sister's keeping-shrine; her limit's trail shifts from red to `RIME` teal.

### 3.5 Emrys Tal — the burning boy
- **Age/class:** 14. Ashwright (black magic glass-cannon; limit: "Everything I Have" — damage scales with memories currently spent).
- **Silhouette:** small and swallowed by an adult scholar's coat, sleeves rolled six times; a floating orrery of three glasspetal rings orbits him when casting. Reads as a child-shape inside a bell of light.
- **Palette:** charcoal `#26221E`, ember-orange lining `#FF6B2B`, chalk-white hair (cost of magic), ink-stained fingers.
- **Weapon:** "Primer", a hornbook-grimoire he has re-written from memory four times — each copy shorter.
- **Wound:** a prodigy who burned too hot too young: he has already spent his mother's face. He keeps a charcoal portrait he can no longer verify.
- **Want vs need:** he wants to *matter now* — to be the strongest, before he runs out; the party needs him to hold back, to still be someone at the end of the road. Every fight, the player feels it: his kit is strongest when he spends what he cannot afford.
- **Speech register:** too-precise vocabulary, tutor's cadence, cracks into a kid when excited or scared. *"Combustion is trivial; containment is the discipline. …Did you see it, though? Did you see how big it was?"*
- **Arc:** I–II: showing off, secretly terrified. III: learns from Seren to "borrow small" — casting off shared party memories, the mechanic's story mirror. IV: offers Veyre everything he has left to undo the Stilling; Veyre — kindly, terribly — refuses. V: casts the great sigil of the rite from the party's pooled memories and keeps his own; on the final screen he recognises the portrait.

### 3.6 Yshara Vhek — the wyldcaller in exile
- **Age/class:** 33. Wyldcaller (dragoon-lancer and the party's esper-bond conduit; limit: sky-fall dive with summoned wind-shear wings).
- **Silhouette:** the tallest of the six; long-limbed, storm-coloured feather-mantle over ashland leathers, spear taller than herself carried across the shoulders like a yoke. Reads as a spear standing in a cloak.
- **Palette:** ash-violet `#5C4A66`, bone `#D9D2C4`, ember-fleck `#C9403A` accents in the mantle, `ZEPHYR` green `#8FE6A0` tattoo-lines that light when an Esper answers.
- **Weapon:** "Long Answer", a rib-bone lance from the first Esper her tribe ever mourned.
- **Wound:** exiled for bonding an Esper the tribe held sacred-untouchable; her exile-name, Vhek, means "the question". Her birth-name went with her banishment — no one living remembers it.
- **Want vs need:** she wants the Espers *freed* from all human binding — including the party's; the party needs six bound Espers as the rite's choir. She walks with them toward the thing she hates.
- **Speech register:** aphoristic, unhurried, second-person plural for the party ("you-together"); calls Espers "the elder kin". *"You-together keep asking what the wind wants. Wrong kin, wrong question. Ask what it is owed."*
- **Arc:** III: joins as the doubter proven right. IV: the pivot — the Espers *choose* to sing, and she learns binding can be covenant, not chain. V: leads the choir; in the epilogue an Esper speaks her birth-name, remembered all along by the elder kin.

## 4. The antagonist — Lord-Cantor Casimir Veyre

**Role:** First Voice of the Cantorate, 58. Not a usurper — the empire's
most beloved servant, architect of the harvest reforms he now means to end.

**The sympathy.** Veyre's daughter, Liesel, was a Choir soloist who gave
her memories to the engines across ten years of civic honours — voluntarily,
lawfully, *celebrated*, while her father signed the citations. She is alive
in his house: polite, healthy, and hollow, greeting him each morning as
"sir". Veyre has done the arithmetic the heroes refuse: the wind does not
give anything it has not taken from someone, the ledger has never once
balanced, and every generation will re-invent a noble reason to keep
spending other people's souls — he knows, because he wrote this
generation's. Stilling the wind is not nihilism; it is a father
amputating an addiction on behalf of a species that cannot. He is
courteous, keeps every promise he makes, protects the party more than
once (a world that loses its heroes to soldiers, he says, deserves the
lesson), and his final plea before the last battle is genuinely hard to
refuse: *"You would keep magic alive because it is beautiful. So was she."*
He fights the aubade not out of malice but terror of the almost: a
half-finished rite means a mad, waking god. If the rite falters mid-song
he is, briefly, *right*, and the final phase is party and Veyre against
the unravelling dream before the song resumes. He does not survive; he is
mourned; Liesel, in the epilogue, keeps his portrait and has begun — 
slowly, like Seren — to fill.

## 5. The eight Espers

Espers are the surviving great dreams of Lunareth — coherent enough to be
met, mourned enough to be kind. Summoning opens a glasspetal vortex
(`esper-portal`) into the Ring; each sequence is a hero cinematic on the
esper camera pose (ART_BIBLE §5.3, 18–21 mm, low angle, symmetry allowed).

| esper | domain | element | summon sequence (one line) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cindrath, the Pyre Stag** | hearth, grief, renewal | fire | An antlered silhouette walks out of a collapsing bonfire, and the battlefield's petals ignite into a spiral crown before the antler-sweep detonates. |
| **Seliora of the Still Lake** | stillness, preservation | ice | The ground mirrors into black lake-glass; a crowned figure rises beneath the enemy's reflection and the reflection freezes first. |
| **Adjura, the Tribunal** | judgement, oaths | lightning | Three verdict-bells descend and toll; on the third toll the sky delivers its sentence as a single column of white-violet fire. |
| **Undalis, the Drowned Cathedral** | memory's weight, the deep | water | A cathedral surfaces impossibly from beneath the terrain, bells flooding, and its rose window bursts into a tidal lens that crushes the field. |
| **Orekh, the Sleeping Range** | endurance, burial | earth | The horizon's mountains are revealed as a recumbent giant that rolls over — once — and the shockwave is the attack. |
| **Veyla of the Thousand Wings** | passage, farewell | wind | A migration of glass birds strips the scene to white, and each bird is one cut. |
| **Halcyon, the First Dawn** | beginnings, mercy | light | Night falls instantly, then the game's dawn key (ART_BIBLE §3) sunrise plays in four seconds, and the terminator line across the field is the blast front. |
| **Morvane, the Unremembered** | forgetting, endings | dark | The colour drains from the frame into a figure that is only an outline; it closes its hand, and everything inside the outline has already happened. |

## 6. Six locations

1. **Hallow's Reach** — border harvest-province, Chapter I. *Hook:* golden
   terraced grain under the low teal Ring; every field is edged with
   keeping-shrines — small glass bells holding one donated memory each —
   that chime when the Aetherwind passes, so the landscape audibly ripples.
2. **The Hanging Abbey of Vhel** — sanctum built *underneath* a floating
   isle, Chapter I finale. *Hook:* inverted architecture — bell towers
   pointing down, cloister gardens growing toward the sea below, walkways
   where "up" is a matter of faith; burns upside-down, embers falling into
   the sky.
3. **The Undersea of Glass** — the ocean flash-vitrified by the Sundering,
   Chapter II. *Hook:* an entire sea, waves and all, frozen as green-black
   glass with the old fleet suspended mid-sink beneath the surface;
   travel runs on cut glass-rails, and at dusk the whole sea catches the
   Ring's light like a second sky underfoot.
4. **Virelia Cantorial** — the capital, Chapters II & IV. *Hook:* the city
   is a playable organ — organ-pipe towers, bell-vault plazas, streets
   laid as staves; when the Choir Engines draw breath the entire city
   sounds a chord and every window's soul-glass glows in section order.
5. **The Emberfall Ashlands** — Yshara's exile country, Chapters III–IV.
   *Hook:* a burning shard-fragment hangs permanently overhead, so it
   snows fire — slow drifting embers by day, and the tribes build in
   long-shadowed canyon shade, roofing their roads with rain-glass.
6. **Moonwake, the Wound** — the drill-site inside the Vesper Shard,
   Chapter V. *Hook:* descending *into* the moon: canyon walls of fossil
   dream-imagery (faces, orchards, cities that never existed) lit from
   within, the drill hanging in the shaft like a cathedral-sized organ
   pipe, glasspetals falling *upward* toward the dying light at the core.

## 7. Register notes for all writers

- Melancholy is earned through specificity: cost is always shown as a
  *particular* lost thing (a recipe, a face, a name), never as abstract
  "life force".
- No one in Erevane says "mana", "XP", or "quest". In-world terms: anima,
  the wind, petalfall, the levy, keeping-shrines, the elder kin.
- Humour is allowed and required (Kite, Bramm, Emrys) — grief without
  relief goes numb.
- Nobody is stupid: Veyre's arguments are never strawmanned, and the
  party wins by synthesis, not by proving him wrong.
